KV Cache · Attention mechanisms

Every token a model writes
adds to its backpack.

A heavier backpack → slower generation, more GPU memory, higher cost.
MQA, GQA, and MLA are three ways to lighten it.

First step

What are Q, K, and V?

Before writing the next token, the model looks back at earlier tokens.

Each previous token’s K and V are saved for reuse. These stored values are the KV cache—the backpack.

Here is the problem

A model has many attention “heads.”

A head is a reader looking at the same sentence from a different angle.
In the examples below, assume 8 heads.

8 heads × every token × thousands of tokens = a backpack too big to carry.
So researchers found ways to store less K and V.

MHA

2017 · The original

Every head carries its own K/V.

Backpack weight
8 / 8

Full head-specific information, but the heaviest cache.

MQA

2019 · One copy for everyone

8 heads share just 1 K/V pair.

Backpack weight
1 / 8

One-eighth of the weight, but sharing can reduce quality.

GQA

2023 · Share within groups

A compromise: divide the heads into groups, each sharing one K/V pair.

Backpack weight
2 / 8

Lighter, with quality close to MHA; used by many open models.

MLA

2024 · Compress, then reconstruct

Store a compact representation and reconstruct K/V when needed.

Backpack weight
< 1 / 8

The cache can be smaller than MQA’s, with quality close to MHA. The tradeoff is more involved computation and implementation.
