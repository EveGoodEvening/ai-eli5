How an AI learns language

The cat sat on the  

One guessing game, played about a trillion times.

That is nearly the whole idea of pre-training. Everything below is just detail.

Scroll

Step one · the reading

First it reads almost everything.

≈ 10,000 lifetimes

Web pages, books, code, transcripts — flattened into plain text. Roughly 100 million words for a lifelong human reader, versus trillions for the machine.

Step two · the game

Then it plays the same round, forever.

Nobody writes the answer key. The word that actually came next in the text is the answer key. That's why this can run on raw text, unsupervised, at enormous scale.

Step three · the learning

“Learning” just means turning dials.

Inside the model are billions of plain numbers. A wrong guess turns some of them a hair in the direction that would have been less wrong. Nobody picks which ones.

Billions of dials · thousands of chips · weeks to months of nonstop compute

What you end up with

Not a box of facts. A box of odds.

“The capital of France is ?”

Illustrative numbers

It never stored “Paris is the capital of France” as a fact. It learned that those words keep showing up together. Every answer it ever gives is this same next-word bet, made one word at a time.

One catch

Straight out of pre-training, it's useless to talk to.

It continues text; it doesn't answer you. Teaching it to be a helpful assistant is a separate, far smaller stage called post-training — a polish on top of all that reading.

In one breath

Read everything. Guess the next word. Be wrong. Turn the dials. Repeat.

That's pre-training
