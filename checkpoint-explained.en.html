Models need
save points, too.

A checkpoint saves the state of a model at one moment in time.

One training step: the model sees a batch of examples and adjusts its internal numbers. The step counts here are illustrative.

01 / Open the save file

At its heart: the learned weights.

Parameters, also called weights, are the many numbers that shape how the model responds.

Model weights

“What I have learned”

They capture its current abilities.
They are not a bundle of the original training articles.

Extra state for resuming training

“How to keep learning”

The optimizer is like a coach adjusting the weights.
Its working notes need saving, too.

Not every checkpoint holds the same things. Some save only weights; a full checkpoint also saves training state. Loading a model also requires the matching architecture, configuration, and tokenizer.

02 / Try resuming

Power cut?

Saved at step 2,000. Interrupted at step 2,400.

Load the weights and training state from step 2,000, then continue training.

Swipe wide diagrams sideways on small screens. Resuming does not guarantee bit-for-bit identical results; data order, the runtime environment, and other factors matter.
