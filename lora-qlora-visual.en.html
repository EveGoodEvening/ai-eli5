First step

A large model is a huge collection of numbers

Each square is one “weight.”
This model has 65 billion such squares.

The problem

Want to teach it something new?
Change those numbers

Updating them all is called “full fine-tuning.”
It can need 780GB of GPU memory to start—too much for one card.

Solution one

LoRA: freeze the big matrix,
add two small ones

A tall, narrow matrix × a short, wide one
produces an update the size of the original matrix, with over 99% fewer trainable numbers.

Solution two

Q = quantization:
shrink each number

Like saving a high-resolution picture as a smaller one:
a little less detail, just 1/4 of the size.

Put them together

QLoRA = a compressed model + small adapters

Three terms
Fine-tuningTake a pretrained model and teach it additional skills.
LoRAFreeze the large model, add two small matrices alongside it, and train only those matrices.
QLoRAQuantize the frozen base model to 4 bits, then apply LoRA. This can make training fit on a single GPU.

Compression loses a little numerical precision,
but the resulting fine-tuned quality can match the uncompressed approach.
