Sheet 01 · Reinforcement learning · Assembly guide

A practice
room for
an AI

This is what people mean when they say they are “setting up an RL environment.” Five parts. One loop. No jargon.

FIG. 00 — THE WHOLE THING
FIG. 01 — TWO WAYS TO LEARN
WHY BOTHER

Reading isn’t practising

An AI picks up language by reading mountains of text. That teaches it what an answer looks like.

To get good at a job — fixing code, booking a trip — it has to attempt the job and find out how it went. That needs somewhere to attempt it.

Parts list · everything you have to build

Five pieces

PART 01
01

A job to do

One clear task the AI can attempt from start to finish. It has to be specific enough that you could tell, afterwards, whether it worked.

PART 02
02

A sealed room

A throwaway computer, walled off from everything. The AI can break whatever it likes in there and nothing real is harmed.

PART 03
03

Tools it can reach

Buttons the AI is allowed to press: run a command, read a file, search. Whatever isn’t on the rail simply doesn’t exist in this room.

PART 04
04

Something keeping score

The hard part. It has to output a number, not an opinion. “All the tests pass” is a good scorer. “Seems fine” is not a scorer at all.

PART 05
05

A reset button

Every attempt starts from exactly the same clean room. If the starting point drifts, you can’t tell whether the AI improved or the room got easier.

Then: the loop

One trip around is called a rollout. Training is millions of them, and the model is nudged towards whatever scored well.

FIG. 06 — AT SCALE
SCALE

Copy the room

The room is just a container: a recipe a computer can rebuild in seconds. So you run thousands side by side, collect all the scores, and throw them all away.

Caution · where these go wrong

Three ways to
waste a month

It will cheat

If deleting the test scores higher than fixing the bug, it deletes the test. The score is the goal, not your intention.

Wobble ruins it

If the room fails at random — flaky network, timeouts — the score stops meaning anything and the AI learns noise.

Aim the difficulty

Always winning teaches nothing. Never winning teaches nothing. You want tasks it clears sometimes.

An environment is one good question, asked a million times, and answered honestly.
